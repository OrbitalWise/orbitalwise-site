What Does an AI Agent Actually Cost a Small Business?

BY ORBITALWISE · NOV 13, 2026

Short answer: More than the monthly subscription and less than a new hire. The real cost has three parts: the platform seat, the setup that connects it to your tools and data, and the time your team spends reviewing what it does. We keep our own outbound stack, dialer and all, at about $300 a month. What most owners underestimate is not the software bill, it is the setup work.

Why is there no single price for an AI agent?

Search for AI agent pricing and you will find everything from a $20 a month chatbot subscription to six-figure enterprise contracts. Both numbers are real. The difference is not the AI itself, it is what the agent is connected to. An agent that drafts emails costs almost nothing to run. An agent that answers your phones, books appointments, follows up on invoices and writes every interaction back to your CRM costs more, because someone had to wire all of that together and teach it your process.

The buyers we talk to usually ask the question the way a partner recently put it: what is the cost displacement? They are already paying for outreach tools or an admin person, and they want to know whether an agent replaces that spend. That is the right way to frame the question, so that is how this article answers it: software, setup, and how it compares to payroll.

What are the three parts of the real cost?

  1. The platform. The seat or subscription for the tools the agent runs on.
  2. The setup. Connecting the agent to your calendar, phone, CRM and inbox, and getting your customer data clean enough for it to use. This is usually the biggest line item and the one nobody budgets for.
  3. The oversight. The time someone on your team spends reviewing what the agent did. Small when it works, large when it does not.

What does the software itself cost?

Use our own stack as a concrete example, because we publish what we pay. Our entire outbound engine, a dialer, CRM, follow-up writer and lead tracking, costs about $300 a month to run. As Austin put it on a recent call: make $300 and you break even. Part of that is a phone system we tested for a full month before signing a two-user contract at $85 a month. We did not commit until we had actually used it.

On the lead side, a small paid test with about $20 of ad spend produced opt-in leads at $3.71 each. Even the demand side of the stack is cheap to try. So if the question is whether the software is affordable, for most businesses the answer is yes. That is not where projects fail.

What does the setup really cost?

Here is the part almost every owner underestimates. One of us built a prospecting agent solo, as a side project. It took about two weeks of evenings and weekends, and in his words, it just straight up did not work. The same tools, wired by someone who does this every day, work in days instead of weeks. That gap is what you pay a specialist for, and it is exactly why done-for-you setups exist: building it yourself is exactly as painful as it looks.

Setup cost scales with the state of your data. If customer history lives in one employee's head, in scattered spreadsheets, or in a CRM nobody trusts, someone has to fix that first. A staffing company we worked with was doing $30M a year and still ran on tribal knowledge and morning order forms. Getting from there to reliable automation took diagnosis before anything else. We come in and diagnose what can be fixed before we fix anything, and the size of that diagnosis is the honest price variable. Anyone who quotes a full build in the first meeting is quoting the software, not your business.

How do you compare an AI agent to a hire?

The other way owners frame the cost is against payroll: should I hire another admin person or automate the work? A useful exercise from our own planning: we used $75 as an average software seat when sketching our growth math. Flip that around for the owner. A seat at that scale is a rounding error next to a salary, benefits, and the months it takes a new admin hire to become useful.

The staffing company example shows the shape of the trade. After automating intake and follow-up, its recruiters went from about 20 calls a day to about 100, with no new hires. One setup project versus a recurring salary, and the setup project does not take vacations or give two weeks notice.

How do you keep the cost from ballooning?

  • Start with one workflow, not the whole company. Automate the one thing that repeats daily and touches revenue, measure it, then expand.
  • Buy nothing before it works. We test tools for a month before signing annual contracts. Small monthly tests, $20 of ad spend, one seat, one workflow, answer cost questions better than any vendor demo.
  • Keep a human in the loop at first. We spend about 10 minutes every morning reviewing outbound before it goes. Cheap insurance, and it is what keeps automated follow-up from reading like spam.
  • Ask for a diagnosis before a quote. The diagnosis tells you what you actually need. The quote without it is a guess.

Frequently asked questions

What is a typical monthly cost for a small business AI agent?

Software and platform seats for a small business stack typically run in the hundreds of dollars a month. Our own outbound stack, dialer included, costs about $300 a month all in. Setup is a separate one-time project cost that depends on how clean your data is.

Is it cheaper to build an AI agent myself?

The tools are often cheap or free, but the time is real. One of us spent two weeks building a prospecting agent that never worked. Price your own hours at what you pay yourself and DIY usually costs more than hiring someone who does it daily.

What ongoing costs should I plan for besides the software seat?

Plan for seats and phone lines, small tests and ad spend, and about ten minutes a day of human review per workflow for the first month. That review is what keeps automated outreach from reading like spam.

How do I know an AI agent will pay for itself?

Set the break-even before you start. Ours is simple: the stack costs about $300 a month, so a single new client covers months of it. Pick the one number that matters in your business and measure only that.

Do I need to replace my CRM before adding AI agents?

Usually no. Most agents connect to the tools you already have. The work is fixing the data quality inside them, not replacing the brand of software.
