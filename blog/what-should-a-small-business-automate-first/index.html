What should a small business automate first?

BY ORBITALWISE · NOV 9, 2026

Short answer: start with the repetitive admin work closest to the owner, like follow-ups, missed calls and manual data entry. Pick one job that happens every week, follows the same steps, and eats real time. Fix that first, then move to the next.

Why not start with the biggest AI project?

Owners tell us the menu of AI tools is so big they cannot tell the good options from the bad. Starting big usually means a long project nobody owns. Starting small means one clear job, one person who approves the work, and a result you can see.

How do you pick the first workflow?

  1. List the tasks that take up most of your week.
  2. Circle the ones that repeat with the same steps.
  3. Choose the one where a delay or mistake costs you customers.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know a task is ready to automate?

If it happens every week, follows the same steps each time, and someone can explain it in a few sentences, it is a good candidate.

Do I need to replace my CRM to use AI agents?

Usually not. Most owners get more value by cleaning up and connecting the tools they already pay for before adding anything new.

How long does a first automation take?

It depends on the workflow. We start with a deep dive so the first build targets one clear job instead of everything at once.

Who approves what the agent does?

A person on your team. Agents suggest before they act, until your team says they have earned it.
